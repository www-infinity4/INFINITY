## Established-physics boundary

The following distinctions keep the catalog scientifically reliable:

- Electrons, protons, and atomic layers are not literally red, blue, or yellow.
- An electron is currently treated as point-like within experimental resolution, while an electron cloud describes a probability distribution, not a red material shell.
- A proton is composite and contains quarks and gluons; an atomic nucleus can contain both protons and neutrons.
- Red visible light has a longer wavelength and lower photon energy than blue visible light.
- Red giants appear redder because their photospheres are cooler than those of blue stars; their enormous size comes from stellar evolution and envelope expansion, not evidence that red particles are the smallest.
- Quantum chromodynamics uses the labels red, green, and blue for color charge. Those labels do not mean visible colors.
- Quantum superposition does not mean that ordinary colored shells literally overlap; it is a mathematical combination of possible states.
